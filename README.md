# The Paperwork Engineer

> "The rules aren't the hard part. The explaining is."

You found the code behind [thepaperworkengineer.com](https://thepaperworkengineer.com).

Hi. I'm Faizan.

I trained as a mechanical engineer. Then I spent five years in medical device quality and regulatory work. (QA/RA, if you like acronyms. This industry loves them.)

Today I lead a 15-person QA/RA team at a US medical device manufacturer. I'm also a certified ISO 13485 Lead Auditor.

I've done the 510(k). I've built a QMS from nothing. I've sat through the audits.

And I learned one uncomfortable thing.

Most of this work isn't hard. It's just never explained.

## Why this website exists

Ask how a 510(k) works and you get a guidance document longer than most novels.

Ask what a CAPA is and you get another acronym.

[CAPA: corrective and preventive action. Finding out why something went wrong, then making sure it doesn't happen again.]

So I explain it in plain language. QMS, ISO 13485, CAPA, audits, 510(k). The parts nobody explains on day one.

The website is where all of that lives:

- **A newsletter** on [Substack](https://thepaperworkengineer.substack.com/).
- **Short reels** on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/the.paperwork.engineer/).
- **A free 30-minute call**, if you're stuck on something real.

## Why I'm doing it

Most people in QA/RA learn it the slow way. Alone, from documents that read like they were written for lawyers.

It doesn't have to be that slow.

If one post saves a new engineer a week of confusion, it was worth writing. If a veteran reads it and nods, even better.

Rule: if you can't explain it to a new hire, you don't understand it yet. That includes me.

## What this website knows about you

Almost nothing.

- No cookies.
- No analytics. No ads. No tracking pixels.
- No forms, no accounts, no database. There's nowhere for your data to go.
- Fonts and pictures load from this site, not from Google or anyone else.

It's plain files. Built ahead of time, served as they are.

Your details only go somewhere when you decide to go there yourself:

- **GitHub** hosts the site and keeps standard security logs, including IP addresses, like any web host. I can't see them.
- **Substack** gets your email if you subscribe to the newsletter.
- **Calendly** gets your name and email if you book a call.

The full list is in the [privacy note](https://thepaperworkengineer.com/privacy).
